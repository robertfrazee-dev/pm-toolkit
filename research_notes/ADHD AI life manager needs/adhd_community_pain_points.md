## 1. Why do ADHD users abandon productivity apps?

### Takeaway
The same reasons come up across sources. Novelty wears off within weeks ("app hopping"). Setup and upkeep cost too much. A growing red pile of overdue items turns the app into a source of shame, so people avoid it. Captured tasks stay hidden until the app is opened. Rigid time-slot planning fails. These claims are consistent but mostly anecdotal or from vendors. I found no peer-reviewed retention data.

### Cited Findings
- **Novelty and app hopping:** setting up a new app gives a short dopamine boost; the app stops holding attention within a few weeks, becomes "invisible," and the user goes looking for the next tool (paraphrase; vendor blog) — [Thawly: ADHD App Hopping](https://thawly.ai/overcome/adhd-app-hopping-productivity-tools); [Kinmory: The ADHD App Graveyard](https://www.kinmory.ai/blog/adhd_app_graveyard)
- **Overdue guilt pile:** an app that fills with overdue notifications becomes "a monument to failures," which pushes the user to avoid it (snippet/paraphrase) — [MyWeirdPrompts ep. #2030: Why Your Productivity Apps Become Digital Guilt](https://www.myweirdprompts.com/episode/productivity-apps-adhd-graveyard/)
- Missing a deadline "triggers a shame spiral that makes you avoid the app entirely" (snippet) — [Sprout: ADHD Apps guide](https://www.sproutapp.tech/blog/adhd-apps-guide)
- The cycle works as "a shame engine": the shame it produces makes worse the same executive dysfunction the app was meant to help (snippet/paraphrase) — [ADHD App Reviews: When You Have Tried Every Productivity App](https://adhdappreviews.com/posts/when-you-have-tried-every-productivity-app-and-still-cannot-function)
- **Setup burden:** heavy setup "uses up the energy meant for the habit itself," and capture systems "multiply faster than they resolve" (paraphrase; vendor; no study cited) — [Kinmory](https://www.kinmory.ai/blog/adhd_app_graveyard)
- **Rigid scheduling:** one ADHD writer dropped detailed time-blocked planning because "that approach doesn't work for me. My brain doesn't function like that" (snippet). They switched to daily goals with no set times and kept going back to the iPhone's default apps — via search summary of [Jezz Substack: why popular productivity advice didn't (work)](https://jezz.substack.com/p/why-popular-productivity-advice-didnt)
- **Motivation gating:** "if I'm not motivated, I simply can't make myself do something" (snippet, same author) — [Jezz Substack](https://jezz.substack.com/p/why-popular-productivity-advice-didnt)
- **Built for neurotypicals:** a developer with ADHD and ASD said productivity books and videos "are primarily designed for the 'neurotypical' individual" (snippet). A commenter said apps are generally too overwhelming (paraphrase) — [DEV: I quit my job and am building a productivity app for ADHD](https://dev.to/buditanrim/i-quit-my-job-and-building-a-productivity-app-for-adhd-i-need-your-input-c8i)
- **Subscription trap:** a reviewer criticized a timer app's subscription because they would never remember to cancel it (paraphrase) — via search summary of [Healthline ADHD apps](https://www.healthline.com/health/adhd/top-iphone-android-apps%232) / [Bean Better Substack](https://beanbetter.substack.com/p/my-top-5-android-apps-for-staying-focused) (the summary did not say which source this came from)
- **Out of sight, out of mind:** "the gap between capture and action is where tasks get lost." Apple Reminders is "an outstanding inbox and a poor engine" with no "push to start" (snippets; vendor blog) — [Focusmo: Apple Reminders for ADHD](https://focusmo.app/blog/apple-reminders-for-adhd)
- The Apple Watch face by default "doesn't show the next task" unless you use the Siri watch face, so captured tasks stay hidden until the app is opened (paraphrase) — via search summary of [Pretty Progress: Apple Watch apps for ADHD](https://prettyprogress.app/blog/apple-watch-apps-for-adhd) / [Applewatchology](https://applewatchology.substack.com/p/ultimate-apple-watch-to-do-list-guide)
- **Notification reliability:** Tiimo's "notification system frequently fails to alert users when routines start," per a comparison review (snippet) — [Soothfy: Tiimo alternatives](https://soothfy.com/blog/tiimo-alternatives.html) / [WhatSticks: Tiimo vs Structured](https://whatsticks.app/blog/zara/tiimo-vs-structured/) (the summary merged these sources)
- **Stress context:** in an ADDitude survey of 1,859 adults with ADHD, about one third said time management and productivity problems cause the most stress in their lives (survey date not confirmed) — via search summary of [ADDitude: Best Planners for ADHD Brains: Paper vs. Digital](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/)

### Inferences
- Abandonment looks driven more by **emotion** (shame, avoidance) than by missing features. An app that shows accumulated failure is one people will stop opening. For Fragments, this argues for no visible overdue counts, quiet rollover or "let go" of stale items, and a Today view that resets each day.
- **Out of sight, out of mind** is a structural failure of pull-based apps. ADHD users need things pushed to them at the right moment (watch, lock screen, a reminder that keeps nagging) rather than having to remember to open the app.
- The **setup cost** finding supports auto-filing. Removing the "where does this go?" decision at capture fits the stated pain point directly.

### Gaps
- No first-hand Reddit quotes could be collected (access blocked). The novelty and app-hopping timeline ("a few weeks") is repeated across vendor blogs with no study behind it.
- No quantitative retention or churn data for ADHD users of mainstream to-do apps was found.
- Notification fatigue and ignoring reminders came up only indirectly ("reminders that politely disappear"; see section 3). I found no primary data on it.

## 2. What has actually worked long-term, and why?

### Takeaway
Paper and simple lists remain the most-reported long-term tools in ADDitude reader surveys. Apple Reminders with Siri voice capture is praised as an inbox. Structured is the app most often cited as one people keep using, because its alerts are reliable and it shows a visual timeline. Goblin Tools is loved for breaking tasks down but keeps no memory. External cues (timers, alarms, body doubling) are the most consistent advice for time blindness. People tend to combine several tools rather than rely on one.

### Cited Findings
- **Paper leads:** in an ADDitude reader survey of 1,342 people, paper planners were the clear favorite. A later ADDitude survey found 61% use hardcopy planners, sticky notes and handwritten lists, while the rest prefer phone alerts and apps synced across devices (search summary; the 61% came from a secondary summary; survey dates unknown and possibly old) — [ADDitude: Best Planners for ADHD Brains](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/); [ADDitude: Best Planners slideshow](https://www.additudemag.com/slideshows/best-planners-for-adhd-minds/)
- **Why paper works:** one reader says handwriting builds routine and accountability. Another says digital planners set off their anxiety (paraphrase via search summary) — [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/best-planners-adhd-brains-paper-digital/)
- **Reader-named apps:** Trello (projects and deadlines), Cozi (family scheduling), YNAB (budgeting), and FlowSavvy, chosen after trying several planner apps (search summary) — [ADDitude: 15 Apps, Tools, and Gadgets for ADHD Brains](https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-productivity-apps-tools/)
- **Apple Reminders, Siri and Watch:** "the second I remember, I can use my Apple Watch to command Siri to add it to my list" (snippet; ADHD user blog) — via search results for [iMore: Using my Apple Watch to manage my ADHD](https://www.imore.com/health-fitness/apple-watch/ive-been-using-my-apple-watch-to-manage-my-adhd-heres-whats-been-working-for-me-plus-time-management-tips-that-could-help-almost-anyone) / [Medium: Apple Reminders is Magic For My ADHD Brain](https://medium.com/neurodivergent-life/apple-reminders-is-magic-for-my-adhd-brain-be5e36aa6b1a) (the summary did not say which)
- Advice is to capture with one fixed method ("Hey Siri, remind me…") and "keep coding rather than breaking focus to organize" (snippet) — [Focusmo](https://focusmo.app/blog/apple-reminders-for-adhd)
- **Persistent alerts:** Due is effective because "it doesn't politely disappear after one notification" (snippet) — [Pretty Progress: Apple Watch apps for ADHD](https://prettyprogress.app/blog/apple-watch-apps-for-adhd). In iOS 26.2 and later, marking a reminder urgent schedules an alarm when it is due (per a search summary; unverified)
- **Structured:** "cleaner vertical timeline, reliable alerts, Apple Reminders integration," with a lifetime license at $99.99 (snippet). It pulls existing calendar events into its timeline — [WhatSticks: Tiimo vs Structured, 30 days](https://whatsticks.app/blog/zara/tiimo-vs-structured/); [TilTaken: ADHD planner apps honest review](https://www.tiltaken.com/articles/adhd-planner-apps-honest-review)
- **Tiimo:** praised for "superior visual aesthetics and icon styling" but its alerts are unreliable (snippet); about $54/yr in-app vs. about $42 on the web (late-2025 test) — [Soothfy](https://soothfy.com/blog/tiimo-alternatives.html); [Saner.ai pricing roundup](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/best-adhd-apps)
- **Goblin Tools:** "Then I found Goblin Tools and it broke that task into 12 steps before I could talk myself out of starting" (snippet, reviewer). Drawbacks: "No history without Pro, no reminders, no calendar," plus stability complaints (snippet). It is "best for immediate task support, not complex strategy" — [Psychelicht: Goblin Tools Review 2026](https://psychelicht.com/en/goblin-tools-review-magic-todo/); [Saner.ai: Goblin Tools review](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/goblin-tools-review); [FocusHack review](https://www.focushack.io/reviews/goblin-tools-adhd-review/)
- **Stacking tools:** "pairing Structured with the free Goblin Tools creates the most effective system" (snippet) — [TilTaken](https://www.tiltaken.com/articles/adhd-planner-apps-honest-review) / [Saner.ai task apps](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/best-adhd-task-management-apps)
- **Timers for time blindness:** a timer "keeps the passage of time top-of-mind"; setting one before starting "makes a session feel finite." The Time Timer visual timer is named repeatedly, along with layered alarms (paraphrase/snippets; vendor blogs) — [Brain.fm: ADHD time blindness](https://www.brain.fm/blog/adhd-time-blindness-planning-tools-time-management); [Flown: time blindness](https://flown.com/blog/adhd/time-blindness); [Flow Club](https://www.flow.club/blog/adhd-time-blindness)
- **Body doubling:** working alongside someone in person or online (Focusmate, Caveday, Flown, Flow Club). Focusmate's format is to state your goal, work quietly, then report back. "the evidence base is still newer than classic ADHD interventions" (snippet) — [ADHD Living: Body Doubling](https://adhdliving.org/body-doubling/); [Flow Club: Body Doubling](https://www.flow.club/blog/body-doubling-adhd)

### Inferences
- What lasts tends to be either **zero-friction capture** (voice to Reminders) or **reliable, persistent alerts** (Structured, Due). Pretty design without reliable alerts (Tiimo, per reviews) does not hold users.
- Goblin Tools shows real demand for task breakdown. Its weakness (no memory, no reminders, no calendar) is the gap a product like Fragments, which keeps history and reminds, could fill.
- Paper's lasting popularity suggests the value of something visible, physical and low-stakes. A calm, glanceable Today view (widget or watch complication) is the digital equivalent.

### Gaps
- How to ADHD (Jessica McCabe) content and Things/Todoist-specific ADHD testimony were not found through search.
- No long-term (6+ month) usage data exists for any of these apps. The "worked for me" claims come from single reviewers.

## 3. What do people say about AI for ADHD (ChatGPT as an executive-function aid)?

### Takeaway
Self-reports are strongly positive for **task initiation, breaking tasks down, sorting brain dumps, drafting hard messages, and judgment-free "talking it out."** Peer-reviewed evidence is a small 2025 qualitative study (n=13) that supports these uses. Concerns: accuracy, vague prompts that themselves cause paralysis, and no evidence of long-term executive-function benefit.

### Cited Findings
- **Academic (ACM, OzCHI 2025, published Nov 2025):** "'A little bit of a life raft'" is a 7-day diary plus interview study of 13 adults with ADHD using ChatGPT. Participants used it for **task initiation, breaking down overwhelming tasks, organizational scaffolding, and as a low-judgment space for emotional processing and communication support** (from abstract and summaries; full text not accessed) — [ACM DL 10.1145/3764687.3764713](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3764687.3764713)
- A related paper covers AI support for adults with ADHD during life transitions (not read) — [GI Digital Library](https://dl.gi.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/896b4266-1437-4c81-bd00-03838954d2bf/content); a review covers opportunities for AI to support adult executive functioning — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391364619_Artificial_intelligence_for_adults_with_ADHD_Opportunities_to_enhance_executive_functioning)
- **Evidence caution:** a Frontiers (2026) commentary notes the AI–cognition evidence is small and fast-moving, and nothing yet links a mode of AI use to long-term executive-function development — [Frontiers in Developmental Psychology 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/developmental-psychology/articles/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1885225/full)
- A BMC Psychology (Dec 2025) study found ChatGPT-generated executive-function rehab plans were comprehensive but varied in accuracy and adaptability without expert review (secondary summary) — [PubMed 41345900](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41345900/)
- **CHADD (approx. mid-2025):** a member of a CHADD peer support group calls AI "a game-changer in managing my ADHD" (snippet). ChatGPT and Goblin Tools help with "planning, organizing, and breaking down complex tasks" — [CHADD: Harnessing AI to Live Better with ADHD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/harnessing-artificial-intelligence-to-live-better-with-adhd/)
- **ADDitude (approx. 2024):** "many say the chatbot has been a game-changer in their day-to-day lives," with a caution that the free version may give inaccurate or out-of-date information (snippet/paraphrase) — [ADDitude: ChatGPT on ADHD](https://www.additudemag.com/chatgpt-ai-adhd-executive-function-support/)
- **First-person (Glassdoor ADHD professionals community):** "ChatGPT is a game-changer for executive dysfunction. I've been using it to break down tasks, estimate time, and draft difficult communications, but even better - I've noticed major reduction in my cognitive load and mental stress around tasks that used to stress me out more." (verbatim from result title; job coach) — [Glassdoor Community](https://www.glassdoor.com/Community/adhd-professionals/chatgpt-is-a-game-changer-for-executive-dysfunction-ive-been-using-it-to-break-down-tasks-estimate-time-and-draft-difficult?commentId=63f802b9beb936004d594f29)
- **Brain dump, then sort:** a common pattern is pasting a brain dump into ChatGPT, sorting it, and shrinking the first step to under 2 minutes. One guide notes this hits working memory, prioritization and initiation at once. Another suggests sorting by energy rather than priority (coach and vendor guides) — [Life Skills Advocate](https://lifeskillsadvocate.com/blog/ai-for-adhd/); [Mentra](https://www.mentra.com/post/chatgpt-for-adhd); [Accountability Muse](https://www.accountabilitymuse.com/blog/chatgpt-use-cases-for-executive-dysfunction-what-it-can-do)
- **What doesn't help:** vague requests "feel paralyzing" while structured prompts help (paraphrase) — [DakLife Substack: ChatGPT with ADHD when your brain freezes](https://daklife.substack.com/p/chatgpt-adhd-brain-freeze)
- **AuDHD first-person:** "5 Ways AI Chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini) Are a Game-Changer for My AuDHD Brain" (title) — [Tacklemode](https://tacklemode.com/ai-chatbots-audhd/)
- **Paying twice:** one reviewer notes that the subscription does not cover external models (GPT, Claude, Gemini), so users may pay twice for AI access (paraphrase) — via [Saner.ai / Marblism roundups](https://www.marblism.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-adhd)

### Inferences
- The most-cited value is **cognitive offloading plus emotional neutrality**: AI feels non-judgmental, which matters for people sensitive to rejection. Fragments' tone and copy should keep that low-judgment quality.
- Needing to write a good prompt is itself a barrier ("vague requests feel paralyzing"). That argues for AI that acts on a raw, messy spoken thought without the user having to prompt well.
- Chat-only AI forgets and does not remind. Combining AI breakdown with persistence and timely nudges is the unmet combination.

### Gaps
- Direct participant quotes from the ACM study, and Reddit discussions of AI downsides (hallucination, over-reliance, "AI did the plan but I still didn't start"), were not accessible.

## 4. Specific needs: time blindness, initiation, transitions, recurring tasks, remembering what was said, rejection sensitivity and guilt, streaks, capture friction

### Takeaway
External, glanceable time cues and tiny first steps address time blindness and initiation. Streaks split users: they help some but can cause total abandonment after a break, because of all-or-nothing thinking and rejection sensitivity. The design consensus (largely from vendors) favors cumulative progress, flexible or forgiving streaks, and no-backlog resets. Voice capture via Siri or Watch is the most-praised low-friction input.

### Cited Findings
- **Streaks hurt (first-person):** after a long daily-workout streak reset, the author's app use became inconsistent. The visible zero made the shame outweigh the progress. "The streak works until the first time it doesn't," which hits an ADHD brain that thinks in all-or-nothing terms and is sensitive to rejection (paraphrase/snippet) — [Crystal Jackson Substack: A broken streak, broken motivation](https://crystaljacksonwriter.substack.com/p/a-broken-streak-broken-motivation)
- A broken streak is "a visible record of failure" that can trigger self-blame, avoidance and "often permanent app abandonment" (snippet; vendor) — [WellNest: ADHD medication app design](https://wellnestapp.app/blog/adhd-medication-app-design); [Simple: Why streaks make ADHD harder](https://get-simple.app/blog/why-streaks-make-adhd-harder/)
- **Counterpoint:** streaks "can be motivating for some people" (snippet). Alternatives include "best week" or cumulative stats, flexible streaks with skip days, and restart rules — [Make10000Hours: Habit tracker ADHD](https://blog.make10000hours.com/post/habit-tracker-adhd); [Sprout: ADHD habit tracker](https://www.sproutapp.tech/blog/adhd-habit-tracker); [Caitlin McColl: streaks yay or nay](https://caitlinmccoll.substack.com/p/streaks-yay-or-nay); [Cycles: rotation planning beyond streaks and guilt](https://cycles.thoughtbubble.dev/blog/rotation-planning-adhd-brains-beyond-streaks-guilt)
- Several blogs cite a 2016 CHI paper on streak dependency driving people to abandon habit apps. **I could not verify this** — (claim seen in search summary of the streak blogs above)
- **Forgiving re-entry:** recommended designs celebrate progress "without demanding perfection" and send proactive notifications rather than waiting to be opened (snippets; vendor guides) — [Ameliorlabs/Naggr: ADHD apps that actually work](https://ameliorlabs.ca/solutions/naggr/blogs/adhd-apps-that-actually-work-adults); [Sprout](https://www.sproutapp.tech/blog/adhd-apps-guide)
- **Too many choices:** "what should I do next?" can be paralyzing. Apps respond with one-task-at-a-time views (Focus One shows a single AI-picked task with a Done button), Now/Next/Later buckets with an AI "Nudge," energy-matched suggestions, and "no backlog to catch up on" reset planners (product claims) — [Morgen: daily planners for ADHD](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/best-daily-planner-for-adhd); [NowNextLater (Google Play)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.nownextlater&hl=en_US); [Focus One (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6756778795); [Focuzed](https://focuzed.io/blog/adhd-planner-for-adults/)
- **Time blindness:** recommended external cues include timers, alarms, visible clocks, reminders, calendars and sticky notes placed where you will see them. Medication "doesn't 'cure' time blindness" but makes compensatory strategies work better (paraphrase/snippet) — [Lifestack: time blindness](https://lifestack.ai/blog/adhd-time-blindness); [GetMotivated](https://getmotivated.ai/blog/time-blindness-adhd-strategies)
- **Capture via voice and Watch:** Apple documents asking Siri on Watch to add to a list or remind you at a time or place — [Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-kg/guide/watch/apd60099a41a/watchos). ADHD users describe Watch-based Siri capture as the key habit — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/health-fitness/apple-watch/ive-been-using-my-apple-watch-to-manage-my-adhd-heres-whats-been-working-for-me-plus-time-management-tips-that-could-help-almost-anyone); [pete.news: Apple Watch and ADHD](https://pete.news/hellocomputer/apple-watch-and-adhd/)

### Inferences
- Fragments' Watch/phone voice capture lines up with the most-praised behavior. The differentiator is what happens **after** capture (filing plus resurfacing), which is where Reminders fails.
- Avoid hard streaks. If habits are tracked, use cumulative or forgiving framing and never show a "0."
- A calm Today view with one clear next step matches the "too many choices" solutions on the market. Many competitors (Focus One, NowNextLater) already sell this, so it is table stakes rather than a differentiator.

### Gaps
- Transitions, forgetting recurring tasks, and "remembering what was said" (conversation recall) had no specific sourced testimony in accessible results.
- Action Button and lock-screen capture were not covered in any result.

## 5. Willingness to pay and privacy concerns

### Takeaway
No credible survey of ADHD users' willingness to pay or privacy attitudes was found. Market prices range from one-time purchases (Goblin Tools; Structured lifetime at $99.99) through about $42–54/yr (Tiimo) to about $17–22/mo (Sunsama, Motion, Inflow). Forgetting to cancel subscriptions is itself an ADHD pain point.

### Cited Findings
- Prices: Motion $19/mo; Sunsama about $22/mo monthly or about $17/mo annual; Inflow about $22.49/mo or $95.99/yr; Goblin Tools iOS is a one-time purchase; Tiimo about $12/mo or $54/yr in iOS vs. about $10/mo or $42/yr on the web (late-2025) — [Saner.ai: tested 44 ADHD apps](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/best-adhd-apps); [ChoosingTherapy: best ADHD apps](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/best-adhd-apps/); [Zerologinfocus](https://zerologinfocus.com/blogs/adhd-apps-for-adults)
- Structured lifetime license $99.99 — [WhatSticks](https://whatsticks.app/blog/zara/tiimo-vs-structured/)
- "43% of users report privacy concerns regarding personal behavioral data stored in ADHD apps" — **no survey named; vendor source; treat as unreliable** — [Saner.ai](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/ai-for-adhd)
- AI assistants that track behavior, mood and schedule "accumulate intimate personal data" (snippet) — [Neurolaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/adhd-ai-assistant/)
- Forgetting to cancel subscriptions is cited as a reason to avoid subscription apps (paraphrase) — see section 1 (Healthline / Bean Better search summary)
- "AI and ADHD Statistics 2026" exists as a vendor compilation (not read; likely mixed-quality figures) — [Saner.ai blog](https://blog.saner.ai/ai-and-adhd-statistics/)

### Inferences
- A one-time or lifetime option, or clear cancel-reminder features, may reduce resistance from ADHD users wary of subscriptions.
- An "AI second brain" that hears every thought holds very sensitive data. Privacy concerns are plausible, but I found no primary survey evidence.

### Gaps
- No primary data on ADHD willingness to pay, price thresholds, or privacy attitudes toward voice and AI capture. Reddit threads and app-store reviews, which would be the best sources, were inaccessible.
